\subsubsection{Contact gate behavior}
\label{sec:analysis_gate}

The gate $g_t$ scales the tactile logit bias (Eq.~\eqref{eq:casa_logit}).
We compute it \emph{offline} on the training demonstrations used for \textit{Peel Cucumber}.
For each timestep $t\!\ge\!1$, we form $\rho_t$ as the maximum, over the left and right fingertip RGB images, of the mean absolute per-pixel change from $t{-}1$ to $t$ (all channels), divided by~255; we set $\rho_0\!=\!0$.
We then map $\rho_t\!\mapsto\!g_t$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:casa_gate} and the implementation hyperparameters stated there.

On five training episodes (874 timesteps with $t\!\ge\!1$), $\rho_t$ has median $1.73\times10^{-3}$, 90th percentile $6.08\times10^{-3}$, mean $2.73\times10^{-3}$, standard deviation $2.32\times10^{-3}$, and coefficient of variation $\mathrm{std}/\mathrm{mean}\approx0.85$.
Within each episode, $g_t$ spans $\approx0.85$ on the $[g_{\min},g_{\max}]$ interval; the time average $\frac{1}{T}\sum_t g_t$ ranges from $0.48$ to $0.61$ across those five episodes.
Thus most steps keep $g_t$ near the floor, while transients still drive $g_t$ over a large dynamic range.

Fig.~\ref{fig:gate_trajectories} shows that peaks in $\rho_t$---rapid appearance change in either tactile view---coincide with increases in $g_t$, while flat segments keep $g_t$ low.
Fine-grained attention over \emph{which} tactile token to read remains in the content term $\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}$; $g_t$ only modulates how strongly non-tactile queries may attend to tactile keys in aggregate.
